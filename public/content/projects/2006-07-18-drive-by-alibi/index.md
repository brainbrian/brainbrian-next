After [The Autumn View](/projects/2003-07-17-the-autumn-view), I picked up the bass and started a new band in Milwaukee called "Drive By Alibi". The five-piece was made up of singer Kyle Anderson, guitarists Mike Esser and Kevin Lucas, drummer Ryan Bollis and me on bass. Slipping into a genre that seemed to change its name weekly, our sound was simply rock. We pulled in elements from older metal and modern alternative, and each of us brought something different to the music, from metal to big band and back again. We released an album called "Under the Parachute".

## "For Drive By Alibi patience makes perfect"

In July 2006, as the band was coming up on its first birthday, Julie Lawrence wrote [a profile on us for OnMilwaukee](https://onmilwaukee.com/articles/drivebyalibi). The article described us as a "fiercely poppy five-piece" that was "taking its sweet time polishing the details of its songs to a sonic sheen" in a genre obsessed with instant gratification.

We had planned to record an EP, but after getting into the studio for a six-song demo we realized we just weren't ready. Postponing the recording gave us more time to spend on writing and to put out our best possible effort. Adding a new drummer gave us a chance to refine our sound, too. The article called out a couple of our new '06 tracks:

> "Pressure" is a tightly executed powerpop anthem with melodic energy and attitude, while "State of the Artist" is a smooth charmer that is nothing if not an infectious sing-along.

Our goal at the time was to have the new release ready in early 2007. In the meantime we posted fresh tracks on our Myspace page.

When asked what kind of music we played, I honestly didn't have an answer:

> "It seems there's so much music coming out in the pop/emo/punk/alternative genre and I really have no idea what to label our music as anymore."

We also talked about the Milwaukee music scene. The fans here were great, but it seemed like most of the successful musicians I knew had moved to Chicago, and the city had turned down bigger venue ideas like the House of Blues, a Hard Rock Café and the Cactus Club's expansion. The way I saw it, our job as a local band was to help raise the bar for quality among local talent.

## Shows

On December 30, 2005, we played Snapdragon Records' all-ages ["After Christmas Party"](https://onmilwaukee.com/articles/eightyd) at the Mainstage in Waukesha. The packed bill included Eighty-D, I Voted For Kodos, The1985Bears, Until I Bleed Dry, Ceteri, Rainy Day Ollie, Headbox, Koala Taxi, The Gas Rats and Ayla Rae.

A week later, on January 7, 2006, we opened for fellow Milwaukee rockers the Sugar Skulls at Onopa Brewing Co. OnMilwaukee [previewed the show](https://onmilwaukee.com/articles/sugarskulls) as the Sugar Skulls got ready to debut material from their new five-song EP.

On July 8, 2006, we joined 15 other Milwaukee bands, including our friends the Sugar Skulls, for ["Rock Out Against Multiple Sclerosis"](https://onmilwaukee.com/articles/rockout), a noon-to-midnight benefit at Burnham Bowl Hall in West Allis with proceeds going to the MS Society.

Later that month, on July 23, 2006, we played an all-ages show at the Shorewood Legion Hall with The Actual, Attention (ex-The Stereo), and fellow Milwaukee bands Molitor and Big Green Sofa.

Punknews also listed some of our shows on [Somerset's tour with The Felix Culpa](https://www.punknews.org/article/16394/tours-somerset-tours-with-the-felix-culpa).
